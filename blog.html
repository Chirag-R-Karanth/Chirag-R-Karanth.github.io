✒On keeping a notebook I almost never reread

I have three notebooks and a shared sense of guilt about all of them. But I've noticed that writing something down isn't really about rereading it — the point is the moment it takes to think, "is this worth a page?" Nearly everything gets a page. Two-AM debugging sessions, a phone number I'll never call, the exact taste of a good filter coffee.

The pages I do reread are the worst hours. Not because they're deep — because they show that today's "impossible problem" was last week's "will fix after dinner". Nobody believes their own trajectory until their handwriting says so.

I don't reread the notebooks regularly. But I keep buying them on purpose.

— finished writing this, folded the corner, won't reopen it until I've forgotten it.

2026 · 01

✒The case for slow, unbroken projects

Everything I pick up now comes with a deadline I gave it myself. The atomic orbittal thing has no deadline and it's the most patient project I own. It tolerates me re-approaching the same equation three different ways, because nobody is waiting on it.

I think ambition benefits from being mostly unobserved. The projects that embarrass me at first — the ones started "just to see" — age the best. Meanwhile the demo-ready ones get abandoned the week after the screenshot.

New rule, borrowed from gardening: keep one thing growing in a place nobody checks.

2025 · 09

✒Notes from a week of rain in Bengaluru

The city reorganises itself on the first heavy day. Offices half-empty, chai stands shoulder-deep in custom, and the traffic learns a slower dialect. I spent the week staring out windows and collecting the failure modes of every app I rely on — servers timing out, alerts I'd stopped noticing earlier in the year.

Rain is a free, unpaid chaos-engineering experiment on your own life. I came out of it with one working umbrella, three damp notebooks, and a healthy respect for the servers that were still up in someone else's city.

— the umbrella is already gone.

✒Why I riced my Linux desktop

It looks like decoration. It is, and that's allowed — but it's also the most productive systems course I never enrolled in. To make a window manager behave you learn autostarts and daemons and the fact that a lock file is never the thing you think it is.

Then you learn the real lesson: a setup you don't understand will betray you, and the fun is that it teaches you before it breaks for good. My desktop now works because I've broken it forty times in a row.

— current theme taxes the GPU exactly 0%. efficiency.

2025 · 04

✒The number plate that made me doubt OCR

My detection project was going brilliantly until I pointed it at real Indian plates and the text refused to be a font. Regional scripts, sun glare, a speck of mud in exactly the wrong pixel. The pipeline was correct; reality was not in the training set.

I kept it. Shipping a prototype that knows exactly where it's weak is more honest than a demo that pretends every frame is ideal. And honestly — watching the failure is the tutorial.

— it still can't read my own city's plates after dark. someday.
